## Calling, Purpose, Assignment

- You can't work in your purpose without walking in your calling.
- Calling feeds into purpose, which feeds into assignment:
  - **Calling:** the overarching, God-given mandate (e.g. Jesus called to bring salvation).
  - **Purpose:** to glorify God through fulfilling your calling.
  - **Assignment:** the specific, season-by-season expression of that calling.
- Calling is the bracket; assignment changes per season, per time.
- Purpose is not one thing: "I feel called to help orphaned children" is an assignment, not a purpose.

## Life Purpose Framework

- Purpose is a convergence of four things, all held within allegiance to God:
  - **Design:** how God made you.
  - **Preparation:** experiences that brought you to this point.
  - **Calling:** what you are called to do.
  - **Passion:** what makes you feel most alive.
- The world's version (ikigai) maps purpose from self outward: passion, skill, what the world needs.
  - This approach is incomplete without the lens of *whose* you are.
  - Leads to frustration when pursued outside God's will.
- Working in purpose is not the same as being successful.
  - Joseph in the pit, David fleeing Saul: both in God's will, neither looked successful.
  - We need to reframe purpose away from results (Romans 12:2).

## Knowing God as the Starting Point

- Genesis 1:26-27: made in God's image, according to his likeness.
  - **Image:** we reflect God.
  - **Likeness:** we function according to his function.
- John 17:3: eternal life is an experiential knowledge of God the Father and Jesus Christ.
  - Not just knowing *about* God, but knowing his person.
  - Daniel 11:32: those who know their God shall be strong and do exploits.
  - The better prayer: "Help me to know you," not "make me strong."
- Knowledge of God is a growing, continuous experience, never a completed state.
- We glorify God by living according to what he created us to be (Ephesians 2:10).
- The starting point is always: *whose are you?* Then design, calling, passion, assignment follow.

## Transformation Does Not End With Us

- Our transformation is not just personal: everyone we meet is a partaker of it.
- We cannot represent who we don't know.
  - Bimbo's analogy: knowing a spouse deeply only comes through time and consistent presence.
- Moses: his design and passion showed even before the burning bush (Exodus 2:16).
  - But it was only at the burning bush, when he turned aside intentionally, that God called him.
  - Intentionality and hunger are required, not just proximity to the right environment.

## Reflections from the Group Exercise

Three questions posed for personal reflection with the Holy Spirit:

1. Do I know God?
2. How well do I know God?
3. Holy Spirit, reveal how I can grow in my knowledge of God.

- **Oba:** the distinction between knowing God's precepts and knowing his person; wanting the latter, and the relational, organic posture that requires.
- **Ife:** knows God's nature (love, goodness) but recognized a lack of intimacy; the Holy Spirit's prompt was simply "sit with me," not for power or precepts, but for closeness.
- **Eri:** understands scripture intellectually but struggles to act from that place; the gap between theoretical belief and lived, embodied faith; identity in Christ not yet fully settled in practice.
- **Bimbo:** God's prompt was to maintain a consciousness of him throughout the whole day, not just in morning devotion.

## Meditation and the Word

- Meditation is how head knowledge becomes heart knowledge.
- Bimbo's practice: write the scripture, note observations, then pause and let the mind dwell.
  - Thoughts that arise in that stillness are the Holy Spirit speaking.
  - Analogy: food must be chewed thoroughly to be digested; the word must be meditated on to be absorbed.
- Writing while reading helps anchor attention and creates space for the Spirit to respond.
- For those prone to reading the word for others (teaching, preaching): ask the Holy Spirit for a personal life application first.
  - This reorients the posture from outward to inward.
  - Luke 5: Bimbo's image of herself as the boat Jesus stands on to speak to the multitude.
