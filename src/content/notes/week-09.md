## 3 Categories of Believers (Hebrews 11:6)

- Text: "Without faith it is impossible to please God; he who comes to God must believe (1) that He exists, and (2) that He is a rewarder of those who diligently seek Him"
- Three categories:
  - Category 1: believe God exists, but not that He responds to those who seek Him; pray but expect little
  - Category 2: believe He rewards seekers, but don't truly believe He exists; chase prophets and altars for a word
  - Category 3: believe both; this is where results, victory, and intimacy are found
- The 50-keys illustration: if you trust the key is in the bunch, you keep trying; doubt makes you quit at key 3
- Your habits, not your words, reveal which category you actually live in

## Seeking God: What It Looks Like

- Luke 15:8: the woman who lost a coin lit a lamp, swept the house, and searched carefully until she found it
  - Process matters; she would have employed another step if needed
- Seeking is active and sustained, not sporadic
  - Expression: prayer, fasting, extended reading, stillness
  - The disposition is continuous, not just the activity
- Hope deferred (Proverbs 13:12) acknowledged: long waiting can erode belief in the reward
  - Three reasons an answer may not have come: spiritual forces to contend with, a principle not yet applied, or God is teaching something
  - Believing He is good shifts posture from "this is taking long" to "God, what don't I know?"
- "The things that are fighting for your attention will be given to you if God has your attention"

## Intimacy Over Activity

- Genesis 3:8-10: God came to walk with Adam "in his usual manner"; there was already a consistent relational pattern
- Challenge for the week: don't ask God for anything; spend the time serenading Him, just being present
- Intimacy is the foundation; performance and activity without it is hollow
- Apostle Pastor Nathanael: 15 minutes of stillness daily for 20 years; overflow is the result

## Week 9 Assignment: Stewardship Action Plan

- Matthew 25:14-30: the parable of the talents
  - The servant with one talent hid it out of fear; the master called this criminal
  - Talents were given "according to ability"; God gives what you can steward
- Task: go back to Week 5 sheets and build a concrete action plan for each area:
  1. Calling (what has the Holy Spirit revealed?)
  2. Personality: strengths, weaknesses, tendencies
  3. Natural gifts
  4. Skills and talents
  5. What people have consistently said about you since childhood
- For each: assess current deployment, identify gaps, plan next steps
  - Example: gift of singing + calling to bring joy → join choir, sing for the depressed, find your specific people
- Two people can share the same calling but have different God-given strategies
- Fill responses in the journal (not just mentally); this creates accountability and opens a coaching conversation if stuck

## Closing Pastoral Response

- A participant shared fear of hearing God's purpose, shaped by years of church activity without real intimacy
- Response:
  - The fear itself is the first thing to address; it is keeping her from clarity
  - Start by filling yourself with God's nature and thoughts toward you, then move into heartfelt worship, then stillness
  - What you hear in that cultivated environment is not a familiar spirit
  - Surrendering now costs fewer years than surrendering at 40 or 60
  - Story of the elderly man who recognized Pastor Dolapo as the minister he was supposed to become; he had refused to yield
  - Youth is an asset; the earlier you embrace the call, the greater the mileage
